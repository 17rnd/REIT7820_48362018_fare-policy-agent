# Agent Capabilities

This document defines the planned analytical capabilities of the MCP-based AI agent for spatial fare equity and policy scenario analysis.

The agent is designed for transport policy analysts who need to evaluate how fare-policy benefits are distributed across geographic areas and socioeconomic groups, and to compare alternative fare-policy scenarios.

The AI agent will interpret natural-language questions and determine which analytical capabilities are required. Numerical calculations will be performed by deterministic MCP tools rather than directly by the language model.

---

## Capability 1: Fare Benefit Analysis

### Purpose

Calculate the direct monetary benefit generated by a fare-policy change for observed origin-destination trips.

This capability compares the historical fare for an OD pair with the selected scenario fare.

### Core Calculations

`fare_saving_per_trip = historical_fare - scenario_fare`

`realised_fare_benefit = fare_saving_per_trip × observed_trip_quantity`

### Inputs

- origin stop
- destination stop
- historical fare structure
- scenario fare
- observed trip quantity

### Outputs

- origin fare zone
- destination fare zone
- historical fare
- scenario fare
- fare saving per trip
- observed trip quantity
- realised fare benefit

### Example Questions

- How much did passengers travelling between two locations save under the 50-cent fare?
- Which OD pairs receive the largest direct fare savings?
- Which travel-distance groups receive the largest average fare savings?

### Current Status

Core data sources are available.

Historical boundary-zone fare rules still require validation.

## Capability 2: Spatial Fare Benefit Analysis

### Purpose

Analyse how realised fare benefits are distributed across the public transport network.

This capability goes beyond simply identifying which individual journeys receive the largest fare reduction. It examines where policy benefits are concentrated when actual travel demand is considered.

### Analysis Dimensions

Benefits may be aggregated by:

- origin area
- destination area
- fare zone
- travel-distance group
- public transport mode

### Potential Metrics

- total observed trips
- average fare saving per trip
- total realised fare benefit
- share of total fare benefit
- benefit per resident
- benefit per public transport trip

### Example Questions

- Which geographic areas receive the largest realised benefits from the 50-cent fare?
- Do outer-suburban areas receive more benefit per trip than inner-city areas?
- Which fare zones receive the largest share of total fare benefits?
- How do benefits differ between bus, rail, ferry, and light rail trips?

### Current Status

Origin-destination trip data, stop coordinates, and historical fare-zone polygons have been obtained.

Spatial aggregation by ABS areas will be added after SA2 boundary data is integrated.

## Capability 3: Socioeconomic Equity Analysis

### Purpose

Assess whether fare-policy benefits are aligned with socioeconomic disadvantage.

This capability will combine spatial fare-benefit results with ABS socioeconomic indicators.

### Planned Socioeconomic Indicators

- SEIFA / IRSD
- population
- household income
- car ownership
- unemployment or other relevant disadvantage indicators

### Main Analysis Question

Are larger realised fare benefits concentrated in more socioeconomically disadvantaged areas?

### Potential Metrics

- realised fare benefit by SEIFA decile
- average fare benefit by disadvantage level
- fare benefit per resident
- share of total fare benefit received by disadvantaged areas
- relationship between fare benefit and socioeconomic disadvantage

### Example Questions

- Are lower-SEIFA areas receiving larger fare benefits?
- Which disadvantaged areas receive relatively low fare-policy benefits?
- Are the benefits of the 50-cent fare concentrated in high-income or low-income areas?
- How does fare benefit vary with car ownership?

### Important Limitation

Socioeconomic indicators are area-level measures.

The analysis will therefore describe associations between fare benefits and area-level disadvantage rather than claiming that individual passengers belong to a particular income group.

### Current Status

Planned.

ABS SA2 boundaries and SEIFA data still need to be integrated.

## Capability 4: Policy Scenario Comparison

### Purpose

Compare the spatial and socioeconomic effects of alternative fare-policy scenarios.

This capability allows the agent to support policy exploration rather than only analysing the existing 50-cent fare.

### Example Scenarios

- current $0.50 flat fare
- $1 flat fare
- $2 flat fare
- targeted concession fare
- alternative zone-based fare structure

### Initial Modelling Assumption

Initial scenario comparisons will hold observed travel demand constant.

The system will not initially assume that passenger demand changes automatically when fares change.

This avoids relying on a single fixed fare-elasticity assumption.

### Outputs

Scenario comparisons may include:

- passenger payment differences
- average fare saving per trip
- total realised fare benefit
- spatial distribution of benefits
- socioeconomic distribution of benefits
- differences across transport modes
- differences across geographic areas

### Example Questions

- How would the spatial distribution of benefits change under a $1 flat fare?
- Which areas would lose the most benefit if the fare increased from $0.50 to $1?
- Would a targeted concession policy distribute benefits more strongly towards disadvantaged areas?
- How does a universal flat fare compare with a more targeted fare policy?

### Current Status

Planned after the historical fare reconstruction and core spatial analysis are complete.

## Capability 5: Natural-Language Policy Query Interpretation

### Purpose

Allow transport policy analysts to ask policy questions in natural language without manually selecting datasets, filters, and analytical scripts.

The AI agent will interpret the user's request and decide which MCP tools need to be called.

### Example

User question:

`Which outer-suburban areas benefit most from the 50-cent fare, and are those areas socioeconomically disadvantaged?`

The agent may determine that it needs to:

1. retrieve relevant OD trips;
2. reconstruct historical fares;
3. calculate realised fare benefits;
4. aggregate results by geographic area;
5. retrieve socioeconomic indicators;
6. compare benefit distribution with disadvantage;
7. explain the findings and limitations.

### Current Status

Planned as the AI-agent layer on top of deterministic analytical tools.

---

## Capability 6: Evidence-Aware Policy Interpretation

### Purpose

Support quantitative results with relevant policy and academic evidence.

The agent may retrieve supporting literature on:

- fare equity
- flat fares
- fare elasticity
- fare-free public transport
- spatial equity
- socioeconomic transport disadvantage

### Example Questions

- Does the literature suggest that flat fares are always more equitable?
- How do the Queensland results compare with previous flat-fare studies?
- What limitations should be considered when interpreting the results?

### Current Status

Optional extension.

A literature knowledge base may be added after the core analytical capabilities are implemented.
